# Suggested first pilot: Can Jesus still use me after I have failed Him?

Status: suggested brief, not a generated video or approved personal testimony.

## Purpose

Test the complete production path in approximately 60 seconds, 16:9: Adam on camera, the same voice over background footage, one explanatory motion graphic, brief emphasis overlays, and a closing return to Adam.

## Biblical scope

Primary passage: John 21:15-19. Background: John 18:15-18, 25-27. Read and verify the actual passages before drafting quotations. NKJV default. Explain Peter's denial and Jesus' commission in context, without claiming that failure has no consequences or that repentance is unnecessary. Avoid invented dialogue and unrecorded inner thoughts.

## Story direction

Open with the familiar ache of having failed someone you love. Introduce Peter briefly. Show how the risen Jesus speaks with him and entrusts him with caring for His people. Explain the lesson plainly and end with a compassionate invitation to turn toward Jesus.

Do not use an invented first-person memory. Ask Adam for a relevant real experience only if one is needed; this short pilot can work without one.

## Suggested visual coverage

| Approximate time | Treatment | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-10 seconds | Adam on camera | Personal question and connection |
| 10-25 seconds | Historically grounded background scenes | Explain Peter's failure and the setting |
| 25-42 seconds | Restoration scene plus simple sequential graphic | Clarify the movement from failure to Jesus' call |
| 42-60 seconds | Return to Adam with brief emphasis text | Explain and apply the lesson warmly |

These are estimates. Final timing must follow the completed script and actual audio. Do not invent dialogue to fill a scene. Illustrative overlay candidates, not Scripture quotations: "Turn toward Jesus" and "Follow Him again." Select wording only after the script is finalized. A graphic can sequentially reveal "Failure", "Jesus calls", and "Follow Him" as an explanatory summary, without implying that the response is automatic.
